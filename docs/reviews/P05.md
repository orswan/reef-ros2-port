## Claim

The complete REEF estimator of master `e4179f48` (horizontal EKF with
velocity, attitude-bias and accelerometer-bias states, mocap and RGB-D
velocity updates, RC switch, landing reset, plus the P04 vertical filter)
is ported to ROS 2 and reproduces the original **bit for bit** on every
compared value of 50 event streams. No correction is enabled (C1–C6
deferred to R1); C1 exists opt-in for evaluation. In simulation, on
**idealized inputs** (truth attitude, idealized range, simulated velocity
observations from truth, IMU vibration assumption) and with REEF not in the
control loop, the estimate is accurate and its covariances conservative.
One fault case fails with the approved baseline: legacy D1 locks the
horizontal filter out after a velocity dropout (F11).

## Defects and surprises found during P05

1. ROS 2's parameter-file merge silently ignores a later file that changes a
   parameter's type (INTERFACES §3.6).
2. The port was built at colcon's default -O0; it now defaults to -O2
   (results unchanged).
3. The P04 adapter held IMU messages for up to 10 ms; it now extrapolates
   the truth attitude.
4. Two criteria were worded wrongly and amended in separate, explained
   commits: the C1 accounting criterion after its first run showed that
   observations superseded before the next IMU step can never be fused
   (the new wording is stricter per step, `517c24c`); and F1/F11 after the
   h05 parity output showed that the approved baseline (D1) cannot let the
   variance grow, before any faults result was scored (`515987e`). F11's
   error limit was not changed, and the baseline fails it.

The estimator was not the cause. The IMU path of the adapter moved to C++

math), and the estimator now reads `/x3/range` directly. The 20 ms limit is
unchanged. After the fix, here: adapter stage p99 4 ms (max 10 ms), estimate
age p99 8–10 ms; `estimator` PASS 6/6, `faults` 35/36 (F11 only),
`interfaces` PASS, `regress_x3_scenario.sh` 13/13.

Remaining margins: the upstream Python `imu_noise` stage (p99 6–8 ms), and
single callback wall-time spikes up to 17 ms seen once here (limit 20 ms;
p99 stays below 2 ms), which come from OS scheduling under load.
